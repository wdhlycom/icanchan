# Day 7: The Map — Eight Dimensions, One Path

*If there's a way out, what does it look like? Buddhism draws a map. Eight landmarks. Not rules. Training dimensions.*

**Tags:** #Buddhism #Chan #EightfoldPath #Practice #Overview

---

Six days of problem. Today: the solution.

Buddhism calls it the Eightfold Path. The name makes it sound like eight separate roads. It's not. Think of it as eight sides of one thing. Eight dimensions you can start training — any of them, all of them, right now.

## What the eight are

**Right view.** See what's actually happening. Suffering exists. It has causes. It can end. There's a path. Those four sentences are the whole map. Without them you're walking without knowing where you're going. Not impossible. Just a lot of unnecessary wrong turns.

**Right intention.** Point your thinking toward what helps. Not toward revenge fantasies, not toward "if only I had more money." The mind burns energy either way. Might as well burn it in a useful direction.

**Right speech.** Don't lie. Don't talk behind people's backs. Don't be cruel with words. Don't fill silence with noise. But this isn't just a rule about being nice. Every time you stop yourself from saying something that would wound someone — you're training the mind. You're practicing not being hijacked by impulse.

**Right action.** Don't kill. Don't steal. Don't use people sexually. Again — not just ethics. Behavior shapes mind. Steal something and you'll be jumpy around police forever. That's not God punishing you. That's cause and effect. Do the right thing and your mind has less to be anxious about.

**Right livelihood.** How do you make money? If your job hurts people — directly or indirectly — your mind cannot settle. You don't have to be a monk. But you should be able to look at what you do and feel okay about it when you wake up.

**Right effort.** Four stages. Stop bad habits that are already happening. Prevent new ones from starting. Start good habits. Grow the ones you've got. No heroics required. Just steady attention to what you're feeding and what you're starving.

**Right mindfulness.** This is the core skill. Watching your breath. Your body. Your thoughts. Not judging. Not grasping. Not pushing away. Just watching. All the other seven depend on this one. Without it you can't tell if you're actually doing any of them or just telling yourself you are.

**Right concentration.** When mindfulness gets deep enough, the mind settles. Not spaced out. Not asleep. Clear, stable, unshaken. Buddhism calls this state *samadhi*. Chan takes its whole name from this.

## Not eight. One.

Imagine a mountain. Eight faces. Climbing any face takes you up the same mountain. Each one supports the others.

Right view gives direction. Right intention gives fuel. The three ethics pieces (speech, action, livelihood) clean up your external life so your mind has fewer things to be agitated about. Right effort keeps it going. Right mindfulness is the method. Right concentration is the depth.
