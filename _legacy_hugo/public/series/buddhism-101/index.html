The Four Noble Truths

The Four Noble Truths The Four Noble Truths form the cornerstone of Buddhist philosophy. They are not doctrines to be believed, but observations to be verified through one’s own experience. The First Noble Truth: Dukkha (Suffering) Life involves suffering. This is not pessimism — it is realism. The Buddha observed that all conditioned existence is marked by: Birth, aging, and death Sorrow, lamentation, and despair Union with what is displeasing Separation from what is pleasing Not getting what one wants The Second Noble Truth: The Origin of Suffering Suffering arises from craving (tanha) — the thirst for pleasure, existence, and non-existence. What is Buddhism?

What is Buddhism? Buddhism is one of the world’s major spiritual traditions, founded by Siddhartha Gautama — the Buddha — in ancient India around 2,500 years ago. At its core, Buddhism is not a religion in the conventional sense, but a practical philosophy and way of life aimed at understanding the nature of suffering and finding liberation from it. The Buddha’s Discovery The Buddha’s teaching began with a simple but profound observation: life involves suffering. But he didn’t stop there.
