In time Chan divided into five houses: Linji, Caodong, Weiyang, Yunmen, Fayan.

What is interesting is that all five taught what sounds like the same thing, and their methods got stranger house by house.

## The stick

Linji Yixuan, founder of the Linji house, was famous for ferocity. A student asks, "What is Buddha?" He may shout in the student's face. He may just hit him.

This was not bad temper. It was meant to stop the thought before it finished forming, to keep the student from rushing off to find a correct answer to hand in.

A shout carries no information. That is its virtue. For a second the student has nothing to hold on to, and in that second something unmanufactured may be looking.

When someone asks what Buddha is, he usually wants a standard answer he can memorize and pass the exam with. The blow says: stop waiting to be issued an answer. Go and look.

## The cushion

The Caodong house went the opposite way. No shouting, no hitting. Its method was "silent illumination": sitting quietly, shining quietly.

Linji is a basin of cold water in the face. Caodong is someone who sits with you a while and waits for you to cool down on your own.

Silent illumination is not zoning out. It is the opposite: sitting still enough that things become visible, the way a pond shows the sky only after the mud settles.

One fierce, one gentle, and they seem to poke at the same spot: the ready-made answer in your head is not reliable. Come back to this moment and look.

## Which house is best

People ask: so which of the five is the strongest?

That question is already pointed the wrong way. The difference between the houses is probably not who stands closer to the truth, but who suits which kind of person.

A hot-tempered, combative type may need Linji's shout and stick to break the knot he has twisted himself into. A fine-grained type who grinds himself down may need Caodong's quiet sitting to loosen, bit by bit.

It is a little like medicine. **No drug is best in itself; it either matches the illness or it does not.**

This is also why the comparison stops being about rank. A pharmacy does not improve by throwing out everything except the strongest drug. Each shelf keeps a different patient in mind.

## Methods are for people

We are fond of asking whether there is one best method. The best way to study. The best way to work. The best way to practice.

The five houses suggest the reverse. **A method is a tool for a person to use; a person does not have to bend himself to fit the tool.**

As for which is best, that depends on what kind of temperament you have, and there is no hurry to decide.

Which raises a practical question. When you want to learn something, do you move only when someone pushes you, or do you grind along better on your own? And the method you picked - does it actually match your nature, or did you choose it because it looked impressive on someone else?
