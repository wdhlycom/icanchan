Cause and effect usually gets handled cheaply. One word, "retribution," and the conversation is over, as if someone upstairs were keeping a ledger and would eventually come to collect.

There's something plainer underneath that.

![Cause and Effect](/illustrations/Zen07.jpg)

## Planting

The two characters mean cause and result. **What you plant, you harvest.**

Buddhism's version doesn't look like a judge with a notebook, entering debits and credits. It looks more like a way things run. When a certain set of conditions comes together, a certain kind of result follows. Plant a melon and you don't get beans. Stay up several nights running and the body starts to give. Same principle, different scale.

Nobody in this is handing out punishments. The conditions are doing the work. That's a colder picture than a courtroom, and also a more reliable one. A courtroom can be bribed or misunderstood. Conditions just sit there and combine.

This is why the teaching is sometimes described as a law rather than a judgment. Laws don't care whether you approve of them.

## The harvest you didn't plant

This is where people usually object. Bad things happen to careful people. Careless people get away with it for decades. If the structure were fair, wouldn't the books balance?

The teaching doesn't promise that they will, at least not on a schedule anyone can see. It says the structure is consistent, not that it's kind. Conditions produce results, and some of the conditions were set in motion by people who are long gone, or by weather, or by a body that came with a family history.

That's not comfort. It's just a more accurate map than the ledger.

The ledger version at least offers someone to blame. The condition version offers only a map, which is less satisfying and more workable.

## Where it becomes useful

Read this way, cause and effect stops being a threat and turns into a tool. **Want a particular result? Go assemble the conditions for it.**

Most people want the harvest without the planting. They want to be fit and not exercise, want the work finished and not start it, want a friend to forgive them and not make the call. The teaching is blunt about this in a way that is almost cheerful: the conditions are the price, and there's no other counter.

There's a corollary people resist. If you keep getting the same result, the conditions you're assembling are the ones producing it. Intention doesn't count as a condition. Only what you actually do gets into the pile.

This sounds like scolding and isn't. It's closer to gardening. Nobody stands over a bed of beans and demands that they be tomatoes. They look at what they planted, and then they decide whether to plant something else.

Complaining about the sky doesn't get you far either. Today's pile of results is mostly what yesterday's pile of causes produced. The only thing you can actually work on is the new cause you're planting right now, which is a smaller field than it sounds and also the only one that's yours.
