Wang Wei. Given name Wei, courtesy name Mojie. Put the two together and you get Vimalakirti, the layman in the sutra who never left home and understood the whole thing anyway.

He seems to have ground Chan into his poems slowly, over a lifetime.

## The word that isn't there

You can read a long way into Wang Wei and never find the word "Chan." Readers feel it on every page anyway.

Two lines, picked almost at random:

> Walk to where the water ends.
> Sit down. Watch the clouds come up.

You reach the end of the water. You do not panic. You sit, and the clouds rise, a layer at a time. Nothing in the line talks about the mystery of things. And the quiet of it is all there, the quiet of someone in no hurry forward and not looking back.

> Bright moon among the pines.
> Clear spring over the stones.

Moonlight through pine branches. Water moving across rock. It looks like nothing but description. It does not add one word of comment. Somehow it has said everything.

## What he was actually doing

This may be the whole of his skill. He did not take "Chan" as a subject and write about it. He carried a quiet mind into the world and wrote down what it saw.

**The mind quiets and the poem quiets with it. A quiet poem already has Chan inside; nothing needs pointing out.**

Try the opposite for a page. Describe a sunset, then add what it means. That time is passing. That the world is empty. That the self is a cloud. The poem gets louder, and the quiet goes out of it. Wang Wei never made that move.

None of this required a monastery. He spent his later years at an estate in Wangchuan, exchanging poems with his friend Pei Di, the two of them answering each other until the exchange became a book. No dramatic departure, no renunciation. An official career, a garden, and a deep understanding anyway.

Su Shi gave him eight characters: in his poems there is painting, in his paintings there is poetry. Following that, you could add a third line. In his poems there is Chan.

## The ordinary scene

Most people assume that writing "with Chan in it" means saying something obscure and deep. Wang Wei does the opposite. Everything in his lines is ordinary: water, cloud, moon, pine, spring.

A lesser poet would have reached for a metaphor here and closed the poem with a lesson. He lists what is there and stops. It takes a certain nerve to trust a list.

Which leads somewhere. Chan is not in the mysterious vocabulary. It is in the eye of a quiet mind, looking at something without announcing that it is looking.

The scenery was available to everyone. He was the one who was still enough to see it.

---

**The harder someone tries to write something deep, the emptier it comes out. What if the depth only arrives when you stop aiming at it?**
