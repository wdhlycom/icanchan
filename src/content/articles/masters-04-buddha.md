Chan traces its lineage back to the Buddha. But the Buddha Chan points to is usually not the one seated on the teaching platform. He is in a very quiet gesture.

At the assembly on Vulture Peak, the World-Honored One took his seat and said nothing. The whole gathering held its breath, waiting for him to begin. He raised a flower.

No lecture. No doctrine. A hand, a stem, a petal or two.

![A Transmission Outside the Teachings](/illustrations/ill-029.jpg)

Everyone looked at everyone else. Nobody could make sense of it. Only Mahakashyapa smiled, slightly.

Then the Buddha spoke: I have the treasury of the true Dharma eye, the wondrous mind of nirvana, the true form of no form, the subtle gate of Dharma, not established on words, a special transmission outside the teachings. I now entrust it to Mahakashyapa.

That smile became Chan's origin. What passed was probably not a joke understood. It was something moving from one mind to another without going through language.

## A finger, not the moon

The Buddha taught for forty-nine years and left a considerable library. Chan picks the moment he said nothing as its starting point, as if to say: **the scriptures are the finger pointing at the moon, and the finger and the moon are two different things.**

Words can point the way. They cannot arrive. At the last step it has to be mind to mind, like two mirrors facing each other, light going back and forth, with nothing inserted between.

The Buddha's own sermons, by this account, are the finger. Valuable, precisely made, and not the thing.

## What the four words do not mean

"A special transmission outside the teachings" is often misread as Chan claiming to be above everyone else and telling people they need not read sutras. That is not it.

It means that besides the path of hearing, thinking, and practicing within the scriptures, there is a more direct line of transmission. Not bypassing the sutras - bypassing the assumption that understanding the text means you have arrived.

Hence the long list of names Chan recites: Bodhidharma to Huike, Huike to Sengcan, on down to Huineng. Each link is a moment when one person could see that another had actually seen. Not "you have understood the theory." Not "you have memorized the passage." You have had the experience, and it shows.

What Kashyapa actually saw was never written down, and probably cannot be. That is more or less the point.

Note the shape of the claim. The teaching is not hidden because someone is withholding it. It is hidden because it does not fit into sentences.

## The things that do not survive being said

We are used to needing everything clear: a definition, a standard, a set of steps someone can follow again.

But some things do not clarify. Whether a person has understood. The unspoken understanding between two people. **By the time you say it, it has already been folded once.**

So: was there a moment when you and someone else understood each other without a word? At that moment, was language superfluous - or did you find it suddenly insufficient?
