Huineng, the Sixth Patriarch, was born into the Lu family in Xinzhou, in the far south. His father died early. He sold firewood to support his mother and never went to school.

One day, delivering a load of wood, he heard someone reciting the Diamond Sutra. The line was "let the mind arise without fixing it anywhere." Something in him moved. He saw his mother settled, then set out for Huangmei to find Hongren, the Fifth Patriarch.

Consider what he was leaving. Not a career or a reputation - a route, a basket, a daily wage, and a mother who depended on it. The road north was walked, and he arrived as nobody.

## Eight months in the mill room

When he arrived, he was sent to the mill room to pound rice. Eight months.

No interview, no reading list, no seat in the hall. The man who would become the Sixth Patriarch spent his first season in the community doing the least prestigious job in it.

The Fifth Patriarch wanted to pass on the teaching and asked everyone to write a verse. Huineng could not read. He had someone write his on the wall:

> Awakening has no tree. The bright mirror has no stand. Originally there is not a single thing. Where could dust settle?

Hongren saw the verse. That night he gave Huineng the robe and bowl and told him to leave for the south before daylight.

## The banner

Huineng went south and hid among a group of hunters for more than ten years.

Later he came to Faxing Temple in Guangzhou and found two monks arguing. The wind was moving a banner. One said it was the wind that moved. The other said it was the banner.

Huineng put in a sentence from the side:

> It is not the wind that moves. It is not the banner. Your mind moves.

The whole room went quiet. **With that line the weight of Chan shifted from "what is happening out there" to "this mind."**

## No need to leave

Before Huineng, Buddhist teaching spoke a great deal about leaving: leaving samsara, leaving affliction, leaving this impure world.

Huineng did not talk about leaving. He said affliction is itself awakening, and awakening is not apart from the world. **No need to go anywhere. No need to flee. Look directly at this moment, at this mind.**

A man who sold firewood took a religion that loved looking outward and turned it around to look inside.

## A book anyone can read

What he left behind was not a profound theory. It was the Platform Sutra, a book almost anyone can get through.

It says the same thing over and over. Awakening is not far away. It is not in the next life. It lands on this moment, on this mind that is able to notice.

So here is the question the flag raises. You keep hoping a change of surroundings will fix things. But "not the wind, not the banner, your mind moves" is sitting right there. At that moment, is it the outside that needs changing, or the mind that is looking at the outside?
