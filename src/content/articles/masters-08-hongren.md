Hongren, the Fifth Patriarch, followed Daoxin and kept gathering people at East Mountain in Huangmei. The community grew larger than before, said to number seven hundred. People called it the East Mountain Dharma Gate.

His teaching came down to four words: guard the original mind.

![Guarding the Original Mind](/illustrations/Zen15.jpg)

## Guarding the mind

Hongren said that people already have a pure, unmoving true mind; it is only covered over by deluded thinking. **Practice is not going out to obtain some awakening. It is guarding the mind you already have, not letting it wander off.**

The phrasing is almost domestic. Not "attain," not "break through," not "purify." Guard. The way you keep an eye on a fire, or on a child near water.

His Treatise on the Supreme Vehicle says roughly this from beginning to end: the task is not to build something new, but to peel back the delusion covering what is already there and let it show.

## The contest

In his later years Hongren needed to pass on the transmission. He did not name anyone. He set no examination. He simply asked his students to write a verse each, expressing whatever insight they had.

It looks like a gentle arrangement. It was not. Everyone in the community knew what was riding on it, and Shenxiu, the senior disciple and the community's obvious intellectual leader, had the most to lose.

So we have Shenxiu's:

> The body is the tree of awakening. The mind is a stand for a bright mirror. Wipe it diligently at all times. Let no dust settle.

And we have the one written on the wall, at the request of the rice-pounder in the mill room, Mr. Lu:

> Awakening has no tree. The bright mirror has no stand. Originally there is not a single thing. Where could dust settle?

Hongren read it. That night he quietly gave the robe and bowl to this firewood seller who could barely read.

Quietly is the operative word. No ceremony, no announcement, no debate in front of the assembly. He saw what he saw and moved the line in the dark.

## What he actually chose

Shenxiu's "wipe diligently" is gradual. Huineng's "not a single thing" is sudden. Hongren picked Huineng, but not because he was rejecting Shenxiu.

He seems to have seen that "originally not a single thing" fits the original mind better. The mind is clean already. It does not need wiping all day long.

**That choice produced the Southern school, and more or less set the direction of Chan for the next thousand years.**

People today still sway between "effort" and "it is already so." Should I grind away at practice, or just let go? Hongren's line does not reject effort. It says effort should head back toward the mind you already have, not toward manufacturing a new one somewhere else.

Which leaves the old trap. Someone wipes diligently every day, and the more he wipes the more convinced he is of his own dirt. Is the awkwardness in the mirror, or in the thought that says the mirror has dust on it?
