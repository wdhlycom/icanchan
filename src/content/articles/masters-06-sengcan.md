Sengcan, the Third Patriarch, did not leave much writing. He left one heavy poem, the Xinxin Ming, usually translated as "Inscription on Faith in Mind." Its first line is enough to last a long time:

> The great way is not difficult. It only dislikes picking and choosing.

![The Great Way Is Not Difficult](/illustrations/Zen13.jpg)

Everyone says practice is hard, awakening is hard. Sengcan says it is not. The difficulty is in those two words: picking and choosing. The mind picks all day long.

Good, fast, right. This, not that. Like this, hate that. From morning to night the mind runs back and forth between wanting and not wanting. It rarely actually stops.

Watch it for five minutes and the traffic is almost audible. The weather is too hot, then too cold. The coffee is not as good as yesterday. That person's tone was slightly off. None of it matters much, and all of it keeps the machinery running.

So it may not be that the road is long. It is more that the mind wears itself out.

## The knife

Picking and choosing sounds like a small thing. But it splits the world in two: good and bad, mine and not mine, should and should not.

Once that cut is made, you tend to move into one half and start defending it, worrying about it, afraid of losing it. Most of the trouble that follows starts here.

You wanted the promotion, so now every colleague is a threat. You decided this city was the right one, so every inconvenience becomes evidence that you chose badly and must keep justifying the choice.

**Not picking does not mean losing the ability to tell right from wrong. It means not letting like and dislike lead you by the nose.**

## A man who did not argue with the times

Sengcan received the dharma from Huike while still a layman. It was the period of the Northern Zhou persecution, when Emperor Wu ordered monasteries torn down, images and sutras destroyed, and monks returned to lay life.

He did not fight it. He did not make a clever show of hiding, either. He withdrew into Wangong Mountain and stayed for more than ten years. When people asked his name, he would not say.

A man who wrote "the great way is not difficult" lived through a persecution of the Dharma, and seems to have acted the line out. He did not pick, so he did not wrestle with the times. When it was time to hide, he hid. When it was time to transmit, he transmitted.

Ten-odd years in a mountain is not a dramatic gesture. Nobody wrote a poem about his courage. He simply did not add a second story on top of the first one: this is unfair, this should not be happening, I ought to be doing something.

## Too many options

People today make more choices in a day than any earlier century did: which job, which city, which delivery order, which message to open. **The more room to choose, the more tired the mind seems to get.**

The line from the Xinxin Ming lands differently now. It is not that having no options is tiring. It is that this mind cannot put down the business of choosing.

Which brings it close to home. You are staring at a screen of options that never ends, and the more you scroll the emptier you feel. In that moment, what is tiring you - the number of choices, or the mind that insists on choosing the best one?
