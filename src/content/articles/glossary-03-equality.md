"All beings are equal" usually gets heard as a slogan, as if it were claiming everyone is the same. Buddhism doesn't seem to be saying that at all.

A cat is not a person. A rich man is not a poor man. A clever person is not a dull one. The differences are right there, and nobody in the tradition seems to deny them.

![All Beings Are Equal](/illustrations/Zen02.jpg)

## The slogan

A slogan is built to be repeated, not examined. "All beings are equal" repeats well. It fits on a banner outside a retreat center, and it makes everyone in the photograph feel generous. The moment you ask what it actually means, the banner starts to curl at the corners.

Equality seems to be about another matter. The differences stay where they are. Using them to sort people into high and low is what doesn't hold up. An ant, a person, a Buddha: on the single point of all three having buddha-nature, they come out even. **Not the same status. The root of being able to wake looks about the same.**

That's a narrower claim than the slogan, and it's easier to defend. It also survives contact with a world in which cats are plainly not people and never will be.

## The scale

Now, where the sentence is most useful is probably not in shouting it at other people. It's turning it back on yourself.

Someone who is doing well isn't thereby more of a human being. Someone who is doing badly isn't that much shorter. This is easy to say and hard to keep in view, because the world keeps handing out scoreboards: job titles, salaries, the way a room changes tone when a certain name walks in.

At a school reunion you can watch the scale work. Someone arrives late and apologetic, and the conversation rearranges itself around whoever has the best address. Nobody announces the ranking. Everyone reads it off the same invisible board.

The differences being visible isn't the problem. The problem is that the board gets installed in your own head, and then you carry it around and weigh yourself on it at odd hours.

The ant is a good test case. It doesn't chant, doesn't study, doesn't sit. It carries crumbs and dies on schedule. If equality depended on what a being does, the ant would be out of the running. It depends on something the ant never had to earn.

## Set it level

Buddhism doesn't ask you to pretend the board is empty. The differences are real, and most of them took work or luck or both. It asks whether the board is doing anything useful.

A person doing well is doing well. A person doing badly is having a hard time. Neither fact changes what the two of them are made of at the root. **The differences are visible; the scale in your head can be set a little more level.**

That's probably what equality amounts to in an ordinary day. Not a declaration that everyone is identical. More like a loosening of the habit of ranking, which mostly costs you your own peace and doesn't improve anyone else's.

It also isn't a reason to lower your standards. You can still hire the better candidate, still tell your brother he's being unfair, still prefer one novelist to another. The scale is about worth, not about performance reviews.
