Li Ao was a leading Confucian of the Tang and a senior official. He had heard about the Chan master Yaoshan Weiyan, and the name was impressive enough that he went up the mountain himself.

When he arrived, Yaoshan was holding a sutra and reading, and paid him very little attention. Li Ao waited. Then he let go of a remark with some temper in it: meeting the man was worse than hearing the name.

He had climbed a mountain to meet a name, and the name was holding a book.

## The name and the man

Yaoshan looked up and said his name. "Li Ao."

Li Ao answered.

Yaoshan said: "Why value the ear and slight the eye?" Which is to ask why a man would weigh the reputation he has heard more heavily than the person standing in front of him.

Li Ao cupped his hands. He conceded the point.

Notice what got corrected first. Not a doctrine. A habit of attention. The man had come to look, and was still listening to rumor. A person can travel a long way and still be standing in the wrong place.

## Two gestures

Something in him shifted. He asked: what is the Way?

Yaoshan pointed at the sky. Then he pointed at the water bottle on the table.

> Clouds in the blue sky. Water in the bottle.

Li Ao's mind opened. He wrote a poem on the spot. The last two lines:

> I came to ask about the Way. There was nothing else said.
> Clouds in the blue sky, water in the bottle.

A finger pointed at the sky does not argue. It gives you something to look at, and then it is your turn.

Yaoshan's answer had no moving parts. It fit in one breath, and it did not need a second one.

## Each thing in its place

Clouds belong in the sky. Water belongs in the bottle. Each thing in the place it belongs, not twisted, not forced. That seems to be where Yaoshan put the Way.

**It does not sound like a mystery. It sounds like the most ordinary fact in the room: this is how it already is.**

Nothing in the answer tells Li Ao to become someone else. It tells him where he is standing.

Most people imagine the Way as something you have to travel to get. Yaoshan says no. The cloud drifts. The water sits still. Do the thing in your hands carefully, and that may be it. Not the thing you wish you were doing. Not the thing that would prove something. The one in your hands.

Which puts the Way not somewhere else but here, in this instant, each thing in its place. Nothing added, nothing withheld. The test was not whether Li Ao could say something profound about clouds. It was whether he could be in the room he had walked into.

---

**Someone is certain the Way is far away. Could that be because they have never noticed that this cloud, this bottle of water, were it all along?**
