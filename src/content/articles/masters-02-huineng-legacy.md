While Huineng was alive, Chan in Lingnan looked like a thin stream. The mainstream was in the north, in Shenxiu's line: learned, respectable, favored by the court.

After Huineng died, the situation slowly turned around.

It is worth pausing on how unlikely that was. The northern line had the capital, the patronage, the libraries, and the polished monks officials liked to invite to dinner. The southern line had a dead teacher, a robe nobody could verify, and a following among people who could not read. On paper it should have faded inside a generation.

![One Flower, Five Leaves](/illustrations/Zen11.jpg)

Two important figures came out of Huineng's gate: Nanyue Huairang and Qingyuan Xingsi.

Each of them grew a large tree. From Huairang's branch came Mazu Daoyi, and below him the Linji and Weiyang houses. From Xingsi's branch came Shitou Xiqian, and below him the Caodong, Yunmen, and Fayan houses.

Later people called this "one flower, five leaves." Chan, as it passed down, divided into five houses.

The image is worth keeping. One plant, one root, five leaves on the same stem. The leaves do not compete for the root. They simply face different directions.

## Three plain reasons

The southern line's eventual takeover owed less to force or imperial patents than to three fairly down-to-earth practices.

**Not relying on words.** Nobody had to read the whole canon. One line - let the mind arise without fixing it anywhere - seemed to be enough. For farmers who could not read and merchants with no spare hours, this was about the only door in.

**Sudden awakening.** Shenxiu's side said you had to work at it for years, one footprint at a time. Huineng's side said seeing your nature might take a blink. For anyone crushed under "I haven't practiced enough," that came as a breath of air.

**Ordinary people.** Huineng himself had chopped firewood. His method needed no ordination and no scholarship, and it fit in among fuel, rice, oil, and salt. So it grew quickly among ordinary people.

In effect, **Huineng took awakening out of the temple and handed it back to people with ordinary lives.**

## Nobody actually lost

Reading this history as "Huineng beat Shenxiu" does not quite hold up. The Northern school did not disappear.

It is closer to say that gradual practice laid the floor, and sudden awakening broke through the ceiling. Later Chan mostly used both ends: step by step on ordinary days, and then, at some moment, seeing.

What Huineng left behind probably was not the doctrine that sudden outranks gradual. It was something simpler: more people came away feeling that this business was within their reach.

That is a different kind of victory from the one histories usually record. No army changed hands. What changed was the address where people expected to find awakening. It moved out of the lecture hall and into the kitchen, the field, the road.

## The threshold you built yourself

We seem to spend a lot of time waiting for a qualification. Read this book first. Finish that course. Meet the right person. Then we dare to begin.

The Huineng story suggests something else: **the threshold was mostly built by ourselves, and the door was there the whole time.**

Which leaves a question worth sitting with. Is there something you have wanted to start but keep telling yourself you are not ready for? That "ready" - is it a real missing piece, or a wall you built and then agreed to respect?
