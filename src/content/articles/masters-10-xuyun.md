By modern times Chan had grown thin. Of the five houses - Linji, Caodong, Weiyang, Yunmen, Fayan - some were little more than names, and the transmission lines were close to snapping.

A lineage is a fragile thing. It is not a building, which at least leaves rubble. It is a handover, and a handover that misses one generation is simply gone.

Old Master Xuyun reconnected all five, one by one.

![Keeping the Five Houses Alive](/illustrations/Zen16.jpg)

## The empty cloud

He was born as a ball of flesh and only came out when it was cut open. The story comes from the Xuyun chronology; believe it or not. His family name was Xiao Guyan, his courtesy name Deqing. Only late in life did he call himself Xuyun, "empty cloud" - like a cloud drifting in the sky, no root, no fixed place.

He left home at nineteen. For the next decades he walked the famous mountains and great monasteries: pilgrimage, study, sitting. One year, during a seven-day intensive at Gaomin Temple, an attendant was pouring tea. The cup hit the floor with a crack and the boiling water splashed his hand.

He woke up. He wrote:

> The cup hits the floor. The sound rings out clear. The void is smashed. The mad mind stops at once.

## A sound, not a depth

"The cup hits the floor, the sound rings out clear." **Awakening often does not come when you are sitting very deep. It comes in an ordinary instant: a noise, a scald on the hand.**

What Xuyun woke to is the old Chan claim. Buddha-nature is not hidden at some great depth. It is in this moment, in this one sound. When the thought stops and the mad mind rests, it was all there already.

The cup taught him nothing new. It interrupted him.

## The work of patching

After awakening he spent a lifetime repairing things.

He restored Nanhua Temple, Yunmen Temple, Zhenru Temple - one broken-down ancestral site after another, brought back little by little. Harder was the lineage. He inherited all five houses himself, reconnecting the lines of Linji, Caodong, Weiyang, Yunmen, and Fayan, and passed them on.

People usually remember the buildings. The buildings were the easier half.

He lived to be a hundred and twenty. The line running from Vulture Peak and Bodhidharma did not break in the twentieth century, because of him.

What he did, in the end, was one thing: at the moment it was about to snap, he handed on what needed handing on.

That is unglamorous work. It looks like scaffolding and long journeys between half-ruined halls. It produced no verse anyone remembers.

A website, a craft, a culture works the same way. **What matters is not how splendid you are. It is whether, at some point, you put the line into the next person's hand.**

So how does a thread that has run a thousand years fail to break? Is it because one person was extraordinary, or because in every generation someone was willing to carry the job of continuing it?
