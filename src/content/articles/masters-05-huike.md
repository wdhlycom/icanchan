Huike, the Second Patriarch of Chan, was born Shenguang. He read widely and was known as a talented scholar. When he went to find Bodhidharma, Bodhidharma was facing a wall on Mount Song and would talk to no one.

Shenguang stood in the snow outside the cave. All night. By morning the snow was past his knees. Legend adds that he cut off his own arm to show how urgently he wanted the teaching, though later scholarship has doubts about the arm. What survived is the exchange.

Shenguang said: My mind is not at peace. Please, Master, set it at rest.

Bodhidharma said: Bring me that unsettled mind, and I will set it at rest for you.

Shenguang looked inward. He searched for a long time and said: I cannot find that mind.

Bodhidharma said: I have already set it at rest.

Three sentences, and the most widely repeated instruction in Chan. Shenguang understood on the spot, changed his name to Huike, and later became the Second Patriarch.

## What "cannot be found" means

"Seeking the mind, it cannot be found" does not mean the mind is empty of content. It means the unease was never something you could grab and put on the table.

People say things like "I have a knot sitting in my chest" or "my anxiety is right here." It feels as if a heavy object is lodged inside, and if you could lift it out you would relax. Search inward, though, and what you mostly find is a flock of thoughts drifting past. The knot itself is not there.

The unease is not in a fixed place either. It is just thoughts moving. **When the thoughts stop, the unease thins - not because someone removed it, but because it never had roots.**

## Not comfort

Bodhidharma's method was not comfort. It was not analysis. It was certainly not "don't think too much," delivered and done.

He sent the man to look. And when the man could not find it, that was the settling. The empty place where the search fails is the place where it settles. The mind was empty and steady to begin with; running after the unease is what made it look solid.

No one can hand you peace of mind. They can only ask you to go and look for the thing you are so sure is there.

## Wine shops and butcher stalls

Huike later passed the dharma to Sengcan. In his old age he was often seen around wine shops and butcher stalls.

People disapproved. They talked. He said: I am tuning my own mind. What business is it of yours?

That sentence is the undertone of his whole life of seeking the mind. **Practice is work done on the inside. It does not need an audience.**

So when you feel unsettled, what do you actually do? Go looking for where the unsettledness is stored - or find something else to do so you don't have to think about it? Which of those is closer to what Bodhidharma meant by setting the mind at rest?
