Huang Tingjian studied with the Chan master Huitang for years. He still felt a paper window between himself and the Way. Thin, unbroken, and in the way. Light came through it. He could not see through it.

A paper window is the worst kind of barrier, because it lets the light in and still keeps the view out. A wall you could at least stop arguing with.

So he went and asked Huitang whether there was a direct method, something that would let him see it.

## A line from the Analects

Huitang did not give a lecture. He took a sentence from the Analects, something Confucius said to his students:

> I have nothing to hide from you.

Meaning: there is nothing I have kept back from you.

Huang Tingjian was more confused than before. This was the Analects. It was not Chan. He could not see what it was pointing at.

Huitang had also declined to invent a Chan vocabulary for him. No new term, no special phrase. Just a sentence from a book every educated man in the empire could recite, which was probably the annoying part.

## Autumn

Then it was autumn, and the two of them were walking together. Osmanthus was open all through the courtyard and the scent came over in waves. Huitang asked, out of nowhere:

"Do you smell the osmanthus?"

Huang Tingjian said he did.

Huitang said:

"I have nothing to hide from you."

## The window

Something opened.

**The Way was never hidden in some secret place. It was in front of him — in the osmanthus, in the wind, in the one moment of smelling it.** Not that the Way had been buried deep. More that he had kept his head down, hunting for a deeper teaching, and never looked up to smell anything.

He had asked for a method, and what arrived was a sense he already had.

The first time, Huitang quoted Confucius and left it lying there, a seed. The second time he quoted the same line with the courtyard as his evidence: I have kept nothing from you. The Way has been sitting out here the whole time.

## Where people look

Most people assume awakening is elsewhere. You have to go into retreat. You have to travel. You have to pick an extraordinary day.

Huang Tingjian's paper window was broken in a courtyard full of osmanthus, on an ordinary afternoon, mid-walk, in the middle of a conversation. Nobody schedules a thing like that. It arrives while you are walking.

You cannot decide to smell osmanthus. The scent arrives on its own, whether or not you have prepared for it. Perhaps that is why it worked.

Which suggests the Way is not far off. The moment may hang on the most ordinary thing in sight, the thing you pass every day and have never once attended to.

---

**Someone searches half a lifetime for the Way and never finds it. Could it be sitting in the place they pass every day, which they have never stopped to smell?**
