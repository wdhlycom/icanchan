"Impermanence" is one of the most basic words in Buddhism, and it usually gets mentioned in the same breath as pessimism. There seems to be a fork in the road there.

![Impermanence](/illustrations/Zen03.jpg)

## Not staying

The two characters mean, literally, "not constantly abiding." Nothing keeps the shape it has right now. Flowers wilt. People get a year older every year. Friendships thin out slowly, in ways nobody marks on a calendar. Even thoughts come one after another, and you can't hold a single one still.

Watch it in one object. A wooden spoon you've had for years has gone darker at the tip, thinner at the edge, slightly out of true. Nothing happened to it. That's what time does when no one is looking.

Buddhism doesn't follow this up with "so everything is miserable." It states the thing plainly: it's all changing, and it can't really be held. **What makes it hurt looks like the not-holding, and probably not the change itself.**

That distinction sounds small. It isn't, because one of those is a description of the world and the other is a verdict about it. Pessimism says: it changes, therefore it's bad. The teaching says: it changes, that's all. You can watch the same afternoon through either lens.

## The grip

Most of the weight comes from the grip. You know the feeling. A good stretch arrives — a week when the work goes well, the kid sleeps, the weather is right — and some part of you immediately starts guarding it. You take photographs of it. You get anxious about when it will end. The guarding is more tiring than the week.

A bad stretch works the same way in reverse. It feels like a verdict on you, as if it had settled in permanently and brought its furniture.

Neither is true, and impermanence is the reason. The good week is already leaving. So is the bad one.

The strange part is that the guarding doesn't protect anything. It just adds a second layer of unease on top of the thing you were enjoying. The week is still the week. You've simply spent half of it worrying about next Monday.

## The relief

Accepting this can be a relief. If everything is changing, this discomfort probably won't stay forever either. And this good patch probably doesn't need to be held with white knuckles.

You still water the plant. You just don't demand that it never drop a petal.

It helps that you don't have to "accept" impermanence first, as a position you argue yourself into. Most people never quite manage it. **See that it's there, then keep living** — that's about enough. The seeing does the work, and it can be done on a Tuesday, in the middle of things, without a cushion or a book.

This is why the teaching isn't bad news. Bad news would be that nothing lasts and therefore nothing counts. What's actually on offer is that nothing lasts, and therefore the afternoon is available, and the person across the table is available, and the argument you were saving up can be dropped.

That's the whole teaching, really. Not a reason to be gloomy. A description of the traffic, so you stop being surprised when it moves.
