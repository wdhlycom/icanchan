In 705 an edict left the capital. It was addressed to a man living in the hills of northern Guangdong, and it carried the names of two rulers: Empress Wu Zetian, then in her eighties, and Emperor Zhongzong, her son, restored to the throne that year.

The court sent a palace attendant named Xue Jian to deliver it at speed. A personal summons from the throne was, in that world, close to the highest honor a commoner could receive. Most people would have rearranged their entire life around it.

Huineng sent back a note. He was ill, he wrote. He asked to be left in the forest.

> The court now sends the palace attendant Xue Jian, riding hard with an edict to invite you. May the Master, in his compassion, come quickly to the capital.

## How the invitation happened

The edict did not come out of nowhere. Two monks were being honored at court at the time, both given the title of national master: Lao'an and Shenxiu, the same Shenxiu who had written the polished verse on the wall at Huangmei and lost the succession to an illiterate man from the south.

When the emperor questioned them about the Mahayana teaching, both declined to answer as authorities. They said there was a man in the south, Huineng, who had received the robe and the Dharma from Hongren, and who transmitted the Buddha-mind seal. If the emperor wanted the real thing, he should ask him.

It is a good story, and it is worth noticing who is telling it. The account comes from the Platform Sutra, a book assembled by Huineng's own community. Rival masters do not usually praise each other into an imperial invitation. Treat the courtesy as plausible, not as documented.

## What he was actually declining

It is easy to read the refusal as modesty, or as a subtle form of power: the man who will not come is the man everyone wants.

But look at what the court was offering and what it cost. The palace meant rooms, patrons, a title, an audience with the throne. It also meant being a teacher of the court, which in practice means teaching what the court finds useful. Huineng had spent decades in the hills. He knew where his work could be done.

There is a sentence in his reply worth sitting with. **He was not only declining an edict. He was declining the sentence in his own mind: "I should go."** That sentence is usually louder than any envoy.

## The question the envoy asked

Xue Jian could see the invitation was going nowhere, so he asked something more useful. In the capital, he said, the great teachers agree on one point: to attain the Way you must practice seated meditation and concentration. Nobody there had heard of liberation without meditation. What did the Master think?

The answer is one of the shortest and most quoted lines in the book.

> The Way is awakened in the mind. What has it to do with sitting?

He then cited a passage from the Diamond Sutra: anyone who says the Tathagata sits or lies down has taken a wrong path, because the Tathagata comes from nowhere and goes nowhere.

Notice what this does and does not say. It does not forbid sitting. Huineng's own community sat. What it forbids is the belief that a posture causes awakening, as if freedom were produced by the arrangement of your legs. If sitting were the cause, then everyone who sat long enough would be free, and anyone too sick or too old to sit would be locked out. **A method that excludes the sick cannot be the whole teaching.**

## The invitations you accept

The scene is thirteen centuries old, but the mechanism is current.

You get an invitation. A dinner you do not want to attend. A position that sounds impressive and would quietly cost you two years. A chance other people would envy, which is exactly why it is hard to refuse. The pressure is rarely the event itself. It is the sentence underneath: someone else would take this, so I should.

Huineng stayed in the hills. It did not look like he was hiding. It looked like a man who knew which place was his and treated that place as his monastery.

So the next time an invitation arrives, the useful question is not whether it is flattering. It is this: **when you say yes because everyone else would want it, who exactly is being invited?**
