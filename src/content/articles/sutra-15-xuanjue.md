Xuanjue had already done the work. He had trained in the Tiantai system of stopping and seeing, and later he read the Vimalakirti Sutra and became convinced he understood it.

That conviction was the problem. Understanding, by itself, cannot check itself. A man can be sincere, articulate, and wrong, and no amount of rereading will show him the difference. So Xuanjue did something slightly unreasonable: he walked from Yongjia on the coast, a thousand miles overland, to Caoxi, with another monk named Xuanze.

He arrived at the hall and did not bow. He walked three circles around Huineng, struck his staff on the ground, and stood there.

> The Master said: A monk has three thousand deportments and eighty thousand fine practices. Where does the Great Virtue come from, that he gives rise to such great pride?
>
> Xuanjue said: Birth and death are the great matter. Impermanence is swift.
>
> The Master said: Then why not embody the unborn, and understand that there is no swiftness?
>
> Xuanjue said: Embodying it, there is no birth. Understanding it, there is no swiftness.
>
> The Master said: So it is. So it is.

## The circle and the staff

The three circles look like arrogance. They are better read as an offering: here is what I have, test it. A student who bows and says nothing gives the teacher nothing to work with. Xuanjue put his understanding on the floor in front of the old man and waited.

Huineng's first move is the rulebook. Deportment, fine practices, the forms of monastic life. Partly it is a probe. Partly it is a reminder that awakening does not excuse you from manners.

Xuanjue is not rattled. He answers with eight words that explain why he came and why he was in a hurry.

## Why the hurry

"Birth and death are the great matter. Impermanence is swift." This sentence is painted on the walls of Chinese monasteries. It is the standard starting point, and it is not primarily about fearing death.

It is about noticing that everything is changing and nothing holds. Your body, the people you love, the version of the world you learned as a child. If that is the situation, then leisure is a kind of forgetting. **The hurry is not panic. It is the recognition that there is no later.**

Huineng takes the hurry and turns it. If impermanence is swift, he asks, is there anything that is not swift? Xuanjue answers immediately. What is realized is originally without birth. Once it is understood, there is no fast and no slow either.

Two questions, two answers, almost nothing extra. The whole exchange takes less time than reading it.

## What confirmation is for

The weight of the passage is in the last line. "So it is. So it is."

This is not a certificate. It is a nod: yes, that is how it is. But notice what the nod does. It establishes that understanding is not self-certifying. Xuanjue could not confirm himself, no matter how well he knew the sutra, and no title could substitute for a person who had been through it.

That is why the thousand miles matter. He was not traveling for information. He already had the information. He was traveling for a witness.

He stayed one night and left. Later generations called him "One-Night Awakening," after the single night he slept at Caoxi. He went home and wrote the Song of Realizing the Way, which begins:

> Do you not see? The idle person of no learning and no doing, who neither cuts off delusion nor seeks the truth.

The line is often read as a rejection of practice. It is more precise than that. Cutting off delusion and seeking truth are both extra work laid on top of something already complete. **The mistake is not effort. The mistake is effort aimed at adding something that was never missing.**

## The person who stops asking

Most people make the same move Xuanjue refused. You read, you practice, something clicks, and then you stop asking. The click feels like arrival.

Xuanjue had more reason than most to feel arrived. He had the training, the text, and the experience. And at that exact point he walked a thousand miles to have it checked.

That is not a lack of confidence. It is knowing how heavy the sentence "I have got it" is, and not wanting to carry it alone. **The test of understanding is not how certain you feel. It is whether you are still willing to be told you are wrong.**

So the question is not whether you understand. It is: who would you walk a thousand miles to ask?
