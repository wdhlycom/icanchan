There is a passage in the Platform Sutra where Huineng takes up a misunderstanding that was common in his day and has not gone away.

The misunderstanding is a sequence. First you steady the mind. You sit, you settle, you stop following thoughts. Then, once the steadiness has built up, wisdom appears by itself. Whole decades of practice have been arranged around that order. The further people go, the less certain they feel.

Huineng starts somewhere else.

![Samadhi and Wisdom Are One](/illustrations/Zen24.jpg)

> Good friends, this teaching of mine takes samadhi and wisdom as its foundation. Do not be deluded into saying that samadhi and wisdom are separate. Samadhi and wisdom are one body, not two. Samadhi is the substance of wisdom; wisdom is the function of samadhi. When wisdom is present, samadhi is within wisdom. When samadhi is present, wisdom is within samadhi. If you understand this meaning, you have the equal study of samadhi and wisdom.

Samadhi is the mind that is not pulled off its ground. Wisdom is that same mind seeing clearly. Huineng is not describing two attainments. He is describing one thing from two sides.

## The lamp and its light

He offers an image:

> It is like a lamp and its light. With the lamp there is light; without the lamp there is darkness. The lamp is the substance of the light; the light is the function of the lamp. The names are two, the substance is one.

Nobody lights a lamp steadily for a week and then waits for the light to arrive separately. The light is what lighting a lamp looks like. A lamp that is lit but gives no light is not a modest lamp. It is not a lamp.

The same holds for the two words. Samadhi is the mind that does not tip. Wisdom is that mind's capacity to see. Ask which comes first and you have already split something that arrived together.

The image also rules out the reverse order. You cannot decide to become wise first and add stability afterward, as though clarity could sit in a mind that is still being dragged around. A lamp without a body is not a lamp either. Whichever half you try to install alone, you are holding a component and calling it the machine.

## The gate people build

Once the sequence is fixed in place, a second error follows. People begin to treat "able to sit still" as "making progress."

Someone sits for an hour and thinks: I was concentrated today. Someone else cannot settle for ten minutes and thinks: I have no business with wisdom. The first has turned a method into a goal. The second has built a gate and then refused to pass through it.

**What Huineng loosens is that gate.** Samadhi, read this way, is not a supply of quiet you accumulate and store. It is what a mind looks like when it stops leaning. Wisdom is not a body of knowledge you gather. A mind that is not leaning already sees.

## The version we have now

The vocabulary has changed. Samadhi is sold as focus: kitchen timers, meditation apps, flow training, all of them aimed at making thought stop. Wisdom is sold as insight: a stack of books, a folder of saved courses, and the hope that one day it will all click.

The two tracks run on separate schedules, and after a few years the person on them is more tired than when they started.

Huineng's point is narrower than it sounds. You cannot install stillness first and wait for insight to arrive later, because the thing doing the installing is the same thing that needs to see. **The one who is trying to concentrate and the one who is supposed to become wise are not two people.** Treat them as two and you have invented the delay you are complaining about.

So the question is not whether you are concentrating well enough to deserve clarity. It is what the mind is doing while it sits there trying to be still. If it is arguing with its own thoughts, is that samadhi, or is it already one step away from the thing Huineng says cannot be divided?
