Of all the words in Buddhism, this one is probably misheard the most. Nine people out of ten hear "emptiness" and go straight to nihilism, to the idea that nothing is there.

![Emptiness](/illustrations/Zen04.jpg)

The full term is *dependent origination and emptiness of nature*. Nagarjuna has a line that puts it bluntly:

> Whatever arises from causes and conditions, that I call emptiness.

## Nothing stands on its own

Anything put together from conditions can't be found to have an independent, unchanging self that belongs only to it. That is what emptiness means.

The table is empty because wood and nails and a carpenter put it together. The "I" is empty because a body, a set of memories, and a stream of thoughts put this "I" together. Take any of those away and the thing you were pointing at is no longer there to point at.

Saying this is not saying the table and the person aren't there. Both are there. They're just not **"just like that,"** unchanged for decades. The table is a process that has been called a table.

Call it a table for the rest of your life and you'll never be wrong, in the ordinary sense. You'll just be using a name for something that keeps not quite being it.

## An emptiness that is not a thing

It's worth being precise here, because the mistake is easy to make in the other direction too. Emptiness is not a substance hiding behind things, the way a floor is under a carpet. There's no secret layer of void stacked beneath the wood.

Emptiness is a way of describing how the table is put together. The emptiness isn't somewhere else. It's the fact that the table is a temporary arrangement, and that this is enough to make it a table.

If emptiness were a substance, you could find it by taking the table apart far enough. You can't. What you find at the bottom is more conditions, and then more, and the regress doesn't stop at a floor. That isn't a failure of the analysis. It's the answer.

That's why the *Heart Sutra* pairs the two so tightly. Form is emptiness, and emptiness is form. The arrangement isn't a veil over the real thing. The arrangement is the thing.

## Two ways to hear it

Heard as "nothing at all," emptiness pushes people toward flatness, toward a gray place where nothing is worth bothering with. This is the misreading that has followed the word around for centuries, and it does real damage.

Heard as "nothing fixed," it lets people breathe. Even the sentence "I'm just this kind of person" doesn't bite down as hard. The person who has been that way for twenty years turns out to be a pile of conditions, and piles of conditions can shift.

The gray reading also tends to make people careless in a way that dresses itself up as freedom. If nothing is real, why show up? The other reading makes people show up more, because the conditions they touch are the only materials there are.

**What emptiness leaves you is mostly a little room to change.** That's a modest gift. It's also the one most people actually need.
