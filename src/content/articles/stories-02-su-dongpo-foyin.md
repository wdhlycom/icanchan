Su Dongpo sat with Chan and got somewhere with it. He also produced one of the better-known jokes about himself. People call it "one fart crosses the river."

At the time he held office north of the Yangtze, and he got along well with Foyin, the abbot at Jinshan Temple on the south bank. One day he sat in meditation, came out of it pleased with his own condition, and wrote four lines. Then he sent a servant across the river with them.

> I bow to the one honored by gods and men.
> A hair of light fills a thousand worlds.
> The eight winds cannot move me.
> I sit upright on the purple-gold lotus.

The eight winds are the eight conditions the old texts name: gain, loss, blame, praise, fame, mockery, pain, pleasure. Su Dongpo's four lines were saying, more or less, that none of the eight could blow him over.

## Two characters

Foyin read the poem, wrote two characters at the bottom, and handed it back to the servant.

Su Dongpo opened it. The characters said: fart.

He was furious. He hired a boat on the spot and went to have it out with Foyin. He had the whole crossing to think the thing over, and spent it getting angrier.

When the boat came up to the temple gate, Foyin was already waiting there. He looked at Su Dongpo, still smiling, and said:

> "The eight winds cannot move you. One fart crossed the river."

## What got punctured

When Su Dongpo wrote "the eight winds cannot move me," he almost certainly meant it. But "fart" is not even one of the eight. At most it is a small disturbance inside blame and praise, the lightest kind of nudge. It blew him from the north bank all the way to the south bank.

Foyin could have written a correction. He wrote a provocation instead. He knew where the string was tied.

So what got punctured was not the sincerity of the sitting. It was the thought riding on top of it, the one that went: I believe I have arrived. A man can sit very still and still be holding a certificate. The more he keeps "unmoved" in his mouth, the easier he is to test with the smallest thing.

## The same test, in a comment section

This still happens, wearing different clothes. Someone says out loud that he does not care what people think of him. One comment comes in, and he does not sleep that night.

Foyin's joke seems to be about that too. **"Unmoved" is not measured at the mouth. It is measured when the small thing arrives, and by whether you are still standing.**

The test is never the big one. Nobody gets tested with a catastrophe they can see coming. It is the email at eleven at night, the sentence in a group chat, the driver who takes the wrong turn. Small enough that you would never call it a trial. That is exactly why it works.
