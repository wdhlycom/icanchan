The apartment goes quiet and you are the only one in it. The quiet is not peaceful. It is loud. You scroll for a while and nothing lands. You open a book and read the same paragraph twice. You lie down and your eyes stay open.

So you find something. You text three people. You turn on the TV. You put on music loud enough to fill the hallway. The room apparently needs a sound running before you can stand to be in it.

![Alone in the Apartment](/illustrations/ill-103.jpg)
Some people turn this into an accusation. If I practiced properly, I could sit in a quiet room for an hour. There must be something wrong with me.

That accusation is not required.

* * *

The panic probably did not begin when you walked through the door. It was there all day, covered by work, by conversation, by the phone. Busy people do not have time to feel uneasy. Crowded rooms do not either. Then the room empties, and the thing that was pressed underneath comes up.

Seen this way, panicking alone is not proof of poor practice. It is more like the first clear look at something that has been running in the background all along — and the discovery of how much of your day goes into using noise to walk around one particular empty place.

* * *

Chan does not seem interested in assigning you a new task called learning to enjoy solitude. That sounds like one more thing to complete, one more way of keeping moving. What it suggests is smaller and harder: when the panic comes up, you do not have to leave right away.

Sit with it for a while. Look at what it actually is. The first minute or two is usually the worst, because the panic has been counting on you to get up and move. After some time you may find that it has no frightening shape. It is a vague unease, an unlit room nobody has walked into. Look at it long enough and it gets less terrifying. Turn and run, and it tends to follow you down the street.

* * *

Some people, alone again, feel it rise and do not reach for the phone. They sit. They count a few breaths. They listen to the smallest sounds in the building, the hum of the refrigerator, wind against the window.

The panic comes and it goes. You do not run alongside it, and after a while it thins out on its own.

* * *

Being able to sit quietly with yourself for twenty minutes is not an achievement, and it does not mean you have arrived anywhere. There are a lot of days left. It is a beginning.

---

**A question to sit with:** That panic that shows up the moment you are alone — how long has it been since you looked at it directly?
