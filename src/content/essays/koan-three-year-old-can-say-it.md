> Bai Juyi asked Daolin of the Bird's Nest, “What is the great meaning of the Buddha's teaching?”
>
> The master said, “Cease from evil. Do good.”
>
> Bai Juyi said, “A three-year-old child could say that.”
>
> The master said, “A three-year-old child can say it. An eighty-year-old man cannot do it.”

* * *

The exchange sounds like ordinary talk. There is no difficult terminology, no sequence of steps to be practiced first and then second. Eight characters, and anyone who can read can say them aloud. It has the same plainness as Huineng's replies.

Bai Juyi's irritation is real enough. He traveled a long way to ask about the great meaning, and what he got back was a sentence he had known since childhood. It would be easy to feel brushed off, as if the teacher had reached for the nearest stock phrase to get rid of him.

That is how the search for depth usually starts. The earlier you heard a thing, the less you believe it works. There must be something hidden behind it.

Daolin does not look for a deeper sentence. He takes the one already on the table and separates two things inside it: being able to say it, and being able to do it. He does it lightly, without much explanation, and Bai Juyi's objection seems to be set aside.

* * *

Put these eight characters into an ordinary day and they are not mysterious, but they are not easy either. The sentence is simple. What is hard is that after you understand it, there is still a long way to walk.

Knowing you should not say the thing when you are angry is one matter. Actually not saying it while angry is another. Knowing you should call home and actually picking up the phone often do not connect. Each of these moments is specific, and each one has to be handled on the spot. Understanding it in your head does not help much.

And these sentences are exactly the ones that are too easy to understand. You heard them as a child, then heard them many more times. Once they are familiar, they are easy to file away as finished business. Since you understand it, you assume you have done it, and you stop thinking about it.

This case has no trap built into it. It does not make you run into a wall. It takes a sentence you are tired of hearing away from your mouth and puts it back into your hands and feet, to see whether you will walk it once more.

**Is there something you understood long ago, and have not once actually done?**
