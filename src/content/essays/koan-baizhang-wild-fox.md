> Whenever Baizhang gave a talk, an old man would sit in the assembly and listen, and leave when the monks left. One day he stayed behind.
>
> Baizhang asked who he was. The old man said, “In the time of Kashyapa Buddha I lived on this mountain. A student asked me, ‘Does a person of great practice still fall under cause and effect?’ I answered, ‘He does not fall.’ For five hundred lives I have been born as a wild fox. I ask you now to turn that sentence for me.”
>
> Baizhang told him to ask the question. The old man asked, “Does a person of great practice still fall under cause and effect?”
>
> Baizhang said, “He does not obscure cause and effect.”
>
> At those words the old man was awakened.

* * *

“Does not fall” and “does not obscure” are one word apart.

“Does not fall” sounds like this: if you practice hard enough, you step outside. Cause and effect no longer reaches you. The line is satisfying, and it is the easiest one to say. Anyone who has done some practice secretly hopes for a threshold, and once across it, the old debts will not have to be settled one by one.

“Does not obscure” says: do not fudge it. Cause and effect stays exactly where it is. You simply stop using it as an excuse and stop pretending it is not there.

The old man's blockage was not a matter of scholarship. It was a small satisfaction hidden inside his answer: the sense that he had become different. Those five hundred lives may not have been a punishment for getting the answer wrong. More likely the satisfaction itself could not find its way out, and so the thing never ended.

* * *

A difference of one word shows up in ordinary life all the time.

A casual “it's all in the past” is sometimes true and sometimes just a refusal to look back again. An “I don't care” is sometimes genuine letting go and sometimes an unwillingness to admit that you still care. From the sentence alone you cannot tell which, and probably only the person saying it knows.

Baizhang does not give a lecture and does not comfort the old man. He changes one word in the sentence. The premise hidden underneath simply comes out into the open, without anyone having to point at it.
