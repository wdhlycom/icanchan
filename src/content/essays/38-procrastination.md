The document is open on the screen. The title is half typed. You delete it, retype it, then go check messages. You tidy the desk. You make tea. You reorganize a folder you have not touched in a year. By noon the document is still empty, and you have been busy the entire morning.

You call yourself lazy, feel bad for a minute, and postpone it again.

![Putting It Off Again](/illustrations/ill-091.jpg)
People who procrastinate are not usually lazy. Look at what they actually did during that morning: a long list of chores, hands moving the whole time. The block is not a shortage of effort. It is starting.

What stands in front of the task is rarely the task. It is a thought: I will do this badly. Someone will read it and decide I am not up to it. It will fail, and the failure will be mine.

Notice that the failure has not happened. It has no shape yet, no date, no evidence. It is a picture the mind keeps refreshing, and the picture is heavy enough that a person will happily take a hundred small errands to walk around it rather than through it.

* * *

Chan has a phrase for the move that helps here: notice the thought the moment it rises.

The moment I will do this badly appears, see it. Say to yourself, oh, it is this that is standing in the way. The thing itself may not be that difficult. Most of the time it is not. The fear has inflated it to ten times its size. Sit down and work for five minutes and you find out roughly how much is actually there.

* * *

Which means the way through procrastination is not more pressure. It is going around the fear.

I have to finish this sets a plate too big to pick up, and the person flinches before they start. What some people say instead is: five minutes. Five minutes is short enough that anyone can sit through it, no matter how unpleasant the task. And by the end of those five minutes you are usually inside the thing, and the rest is something you want to keep doing.

* * *

It works because it does not touch the fear. The fear is about doing it badly, and five minutes does not require doing it well. It only requires sitting down and beginning.

Beginning is where almost everyone gets stuck. The rest is mostly logistics.

* * *

Some people, when they feel the old urge to postpone, do not open the planner and build a schedule. They sit down and work for five minutes. Just five.

---

**A question to sit with:** The thing you have been putting off — are you afraid of doing it badly, or of finishing it and finding that the result is smaller than what you imagined?
