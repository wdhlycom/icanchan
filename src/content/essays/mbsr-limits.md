MBSR, Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction, was put together by Jon Kabat-Zinn in 1979. The people sitting across from him were not looking for a path. They were patients who had been worn down by chronic pain for years, and medicine did not have much left to offer them. He wondered whether something else could be offered instead.

His idea was concrete. Take meditation out of the religious setting it came from, keep only the act of noticing, and build an eight-week course around it. The goal went on the label in plain language: stress reduction. That origin also marks the outer edge of what the course can claim. It was designed to help people feel a little better. Liberation was never on the drawing board.

## An honest goal, met

Feeling better is an honest goal, and the course meets it. People with chronic pain hurt less. People prone to anxiety are frightened less often and less hard. People who could not sleep can close their eyes. In the middle of a life, none of these is a small thing, and saying that they happen is not an overclaim.

What does not hold up is the step people take next, and they take it in both directions.

---

On one side, someone notices that MBSR works and concludes that Chan has now been confirmed by science. On the other, someone complains that Chan cannot produce a liberation you can measure with an instrument, and concludes that Chan is useless. Both moves quietly change the subject. The first treats a result about pain and anxiety as a verdict on a whole account of mind. The second treats the absence of a measuring device as the absence of the thing.

MBSR working tells you that this method helps with certain kinds of pain. It does not certify that the teaching of not-self is true, and it does not guarantee that liberation is measurable either way. It was never built to answer those questions. Chan's liberation was not on that account to begin with. It is not a clinical technique waiting for a laboratory to sign off. It is closer to a set of claims about how this mind should be seen, and claims of that kind are not confirmed or refuted by whether a course lowers an anxiety score.

That distinction is easy to lose because the two practices share a surface. The same posture, the same breath, the same instruction to notice what is happening. A person watching from outside sees one continuous thing, and the course's respectable evidence gets borrowed by everything standing nearby.

## Two tools, two jobs

None of this forces a choice. If what you want is less stress, MBSR is a dependable instrument, and there is no reason to be embarrassed about using it. It was made for that, and it does it. If what you want is to see where suffering comes from, the course will take you part of the way and then stop, because it was never built to go further. Stopping is not a failure. It is the edge of the map.

The failure I worry about is quieter than either of the loud errors. Someone practices for eight years, sits every morning, and does feel better — calmer, less reactive, sleeping through the night. Because the improvement is real, they assume the other thing has happened as well. They take the comfort as evidence of insight. The body is more comfortable, and the position has not moved. There is no crisis to mark the moment, no test that fails, just a gradual substitution of one word for another until the two are the same word.

I understand the temptation. When something finally eases, the mind wants to name the easing as the whole answer, because the whole answer is more satisfying than a partial one. But comfort is a state that comes and goes on its own schedule, and whatever was seen in practice has to survive the return of the pain. A method that reliably produces calm is valuable. It is not the same as a method that changes how you stand in relation to the calm.

Being more comfortable is a good thing and a poor destination. Keeping the two apart is not a demotion of MBSR. It is what lets each one be described accurately: one is a well-tested way to suffer less in the body, the other is an inquiry into why the suffering was there in the first place. Confusing them does not strengthen either. It makes the first do work it cannot do, and it leaves the second one unstarted, which is the larger loss, because the second one takes longer and the years go by whether or not anyone begins.
