> Li Ao asked Yaoshan Weiyan, “What is the Way?”
>
> Yaoshan pointed up with his hand, then down, and said, “Clouds in the blue sky, water in the bottle.”
>
> Li Ao was delighted and wrote a verse: “Training the body until it is like a crane's, two boxes of scriptures under a thousand pines. I came to ask about the Way and there was nothing else to say: clouds in the blue sky, water in the bottle.”

* * *

He asks about “the Way,” one of the biggest words there is. The answer describes where clouds and water happen to be.

Clouds are in the sky. Water is in the bottle. Both look settled, as if neither needs to move somewhere else. Yaoshan does not seem to be telling the clouds to study the water, or the water to envy the clouds. He does not extract a doctrine from the two lines and hand it over.

The difficulty is that the answer is too smooth. It is so smooth that you start to suspect it. Is that it? There must be a deeper sentence held back.

Li Ao's verse is beautiful, but the words “nothing else to say” have already assigned a meaning to Yaoshan's line. Yaoshan seems to have raised a hand and pointed. He may not have had that much to say.

* * *

The mind usually has a position prepared: this present thing does not count; somewhere else would count. The work in your hands does not count; once you have finished a certain project, then it counts. This day does not count; once life is settled, then it counts.

Clouds and water do not seem to have this problem. Not because they understand anything. They are simply not longing for somewhere else.

The water in the bottle does not look wronged. The bottle is a certain size, so the water is that size. It takes the shape it is given. Cool or warm, it goes along. It does not need to become a cloud first in order to be called water.
