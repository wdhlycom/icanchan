There is a stretch of road every day: the packed train, the traffic, the red light that will not change.

Most people file it as waste to be endured, the least meaningful part of the day.

Turn it around. The only time you control, with no one asking you for anything, may be exactly these stretches.

![Bored on the Commute](/illustrations/ill-120.jpg)
People assume practice needs a block of cleared time. A weekend, a holiday, a retreat. So the weekdays never qualify.

That is off by a distance. Chan was never kept on a cushion. It is closer to while the day is happening: standing, walking, being squeezed, waiting.

Hui Neng said walking, standing, sitting, and lying down are all Chan. He did not sound like he was making a comparison. He sounded literal. That sentence is not advice for monks with free afternoons. It is a description of where the body already is.

* * *

Someone tried this on the way to work: no headphones, no phone.

Just standing in the carriage, doing nothing in particular, looking.

Faces: rushing, eyes closed, blank. The announcement repeating. The friction of a crowd moving. The feeling of being pressed in, footing unsure, and the sentence *this is annoying* rising a little at a time.

The point is not to learn to love the train. The point is this. The annoyance, the crowding, the noise come, and you watch them come. They go, and you watch them go. You do not wrestle them and you do not get carried off.

A ride like that stops being something endured. It starts to look like practice in not dwelling, thought after thought, without having to chase any of them away.

* * *

People treat practice as a job that needs a free hand, as if life has to pause first.

Look closer and it is the reverse. Practice is mostly not being asleep while living. Knowing you are eating when you eat. Knowing you are walking when you walk. Knowing you are irritated when you are squeezed, without letting the irritation swallow the whole afternoon.

The commute, the dishes, the meeting, the queue: the stretches called junk time are good training rooms. Ordinary enough, unglamorous enough, nothing worth choosing instead.

* * *

Someone takes ten minutes of the commute, puts the phone away, and just watches. Not calling it meditation, not making it a task. Being there.

After a few rides, the dullest part of the day stops feeling wasted. The train is exactly as crowded as before. Nothing was added to it. Something was simply left alone.

---

**A question to sit with:** Which other stretches of the day do you only want to get through, and if each one were a training room, what would your days hold less of?
