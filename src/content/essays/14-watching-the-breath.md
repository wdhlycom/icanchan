When you are nervous, someone nearby says take a deep breath.

You try. You pull the air in hard, push it out hard, determined to settle yourself. Often it goes the other way. The more you manage the breath, the more ragged it gets.

So the trouble is not entirely in the breath. It is in what you do to it.

![Watching the Breath](/illustrations/ill-109.jpg)
Anxious, you are quietly arm-wrestling your own breathing. You feel you need more oxygen. You feel you need to slow down, to count to four before letting go. A breath that would have walked on its own becomes a task with a deadline.

When the task fails, which it usually does, you panic a little. Panic makes the breath shorter. The shorter breath confirms that something is wrong. The loop closes.

Chan turns the other way. Do not rush to change it. Watch it from the side.

* * *

Sit somewhere quiet. Leave the speed and the depth alone. Just watch: air coming in, the chest lifting, the long slow going out.

It is like standing on a riverbank. How the river runs is the river's business. You would not stand there shouting at the water to move faster. You are not the current and you are not the bank. You are just the watching.

The odd part is this. Stop giving orders and the breath often slows by itself. The grip of *I must be calm* loosens first, and the breath follows.

* * *

Elsewhere effort pays. Push and the barbell rises. Work the numbers and the answer appears. Breathing is one of the few things that gets worse the harder you try.

Tell it to slow and it hurries. Force yourself to be calm and the forcing adds its own tension. It keeps no schedule and no score. There is no progress bar, no two extra breaths compared with yesterday, nothing to report back to anyone.

So while you are watching the breath, you are also watching the person who wants to manage even this. Seeing that may matter more than smoothing the breath out. One is a technique. The other is a look at how you hold your own life.

* * *

When the mind is scrambled, do not reach for a deep breath. Close your eyes and watch ten breaths without changing one of them.

Watching that way, most people notice that calm is not manufactured. You let go, and it comes up on its own, the way a surface settles once you stop stirring it. The instruction was never to breathe well. It was to stop editing.

---

**A question to sit with:** What else in your life gets more out of control the harder you try to fix it, and what would happen if you only watched?
