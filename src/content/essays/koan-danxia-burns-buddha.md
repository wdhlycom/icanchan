> It was bitterly cold at Huilin Monastery. Danxia Tianran took a wooden Buddha from the hall and burned it for warmth.
>
> The abbot scolded him: “How can you burn my wooden Buddha?”
>
> Danxia stirred the ash with his staff and said, “I am burning it to collect the relics.”
>
> The abbot said, “A wooden Buddha has no relics.”
>
> Danxia said, “Then bring two more and burn them.”

* * *

Danxia's hand is on a piece of wood. What he disturbs is the care people bring to it.

A person believes, and belief gradually settles onto the statue. It must be offered to, bowed to, and never touched. After enough time, the wood itself seems to be the Buddha, and anyone who moves it is being disrespectful.

Danxia's fire pushes the conversation somewhere it cannot easily get around. If the wooden Buddha has no relics inside, then it is a piece of wood. If it really is the Buddha, then burning it should not matter. Neither side of that holds up very well.

When neither side holds up, a person may finally ask whether the Buddha is in that piece of wood at all. It seems closer to say that it is in the mind of the person bowing. And the more reverently you treat the wood as the Buddha, the more you may be locking the Buddha inside it.

* * *

Danxia is probably not telling anyone to go home and burn the statues. What he burns is a habit: the habit of taking an image, a form, and calling it the thing itself.

A life has quite a few wooden Buddhas in it. A book whose corners must not be folded. An authority that does not tolerate questions. A set of rules that cannot be touched. They may once have pointed at something that mattered. But left standing long enough, and bowed to long enough, what remains is often just a piece of wood waiting for someone to knock their head against it.

Respect, if it means anything, is probably not in keeping the thing untouched. It is closer to still being able to recognize the living source it once pointed to.

---

**Is there an image in your mind you do not dare touch, or a rule that cannot be moved? After bowing to it this long, can you still tell whether you are respecting what it points to, or just the wood?**
