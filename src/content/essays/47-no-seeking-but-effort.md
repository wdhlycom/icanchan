Letting go comes up so often in Buddhist conversation that after a while the word starts to wobble in your hands. If nothing is supposed to be sought, then what is all this for? Why get up before light? Why stay late? Is the work on your desk still worth doing well?

It is a misunderstanding, and a fairly common one.

What Chan calls non-seeking does not sound like an instruction to fold your hands. It sounds more like a warning against holding the result too tightly. You still make the effort. You still do the work, eat the meals, let the days pass one after another. What changes is how tight your chest is while you do it, and whether your mind keeps running back to the payoff.

![No Seeking, Still Trying](/illustrations/ill-087.jpg)
Baizhang makes this easier to see. He set down the rule that a day without work is a day without eating, and he was still out in the fields in his eighties. His students, worried about him, hid his hoe. He simply stopped eating. It did not look like sulking. He seems to have believed, plainly, that a person who does not work should not take the bowl.

Call him a man without seeking and something doesn't fit. He was the least idle person in the monastery.

* * *

So non-seeking and effort are not really at war. What Chan seems to describe is pressing on with the work while refusing to wrestle with a result that hasn't happened yet.

The doing itself is mostly a matter of carrying your share — lifting what this life asks you to lift, finishing what is in front of you as well as you can. That side of it looks solid, and it is headed somewhere higher, so calling it seeking is fair enough.

Non-seeking is the slow loosening of the thought that says doing this must earn me that. If it comes, you are not carried away by it. If it doesn't, you don't collapse. The effort already went in the right direction, and those days were not wasted just because the outcome turned out otherwise.

* * *

Turn it around, though. If someone decides that seeking nothing and doing nothing is what Zen means, what you see looks a lot more like laziness. It has very little to do with Chan.

Chan does not appear to have spent much time telling people to lie down. What it offers is a way to be less frantic while trying, mostly because you know that once your effort is spent, causes and conditions still have a hand in what happens. Not all of it was ever yours to hold.

* * *

So yes, effort. Only the thing you are aiming at tends to move. Early on it is usually in order to get. Later it looks more like in order not to have anything to answer for.

That shift doesn't make anyone free all at once. But the string that has been taut in the chest for years can loosen a little.

---

**A question to sit with:** Is today's effort about winning, or about not letting yourself down?
