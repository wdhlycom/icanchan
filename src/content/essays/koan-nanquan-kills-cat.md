> The monks of the eastern and western halls were quarrelling over a cat. Nanquan held it up and said, “If any of you can say a word, I will spare it. If not, I will cut it.”
>
> No one answered.
>
> He cut the cat in two.
>
> That evening Zhaozhou returned from outside. Nanquan told him what had happened. Zhaozhou took off his shoe, put it on his head, and walked out.
>
> Nanquan said, “If you had been here, the cat would have been saved.”

Let me say the obvious first. The killing in this story is harsh, and there is no need to imitate it or to invent a justification for it.

People have retold this case for centuries, and not because of the cat. They keep looking at where that one stroke actually landed.

* * *

Two halls quarrel over a cat. What they are quarrelling over may not be the cat. It looks more like the grip of *this one belongs to us*. Nanquan picks the cat up and holds it in front of everyone. He has put that grip on display and asked for one word. Nobody speaks. Which probably means that underneath the quarrel there was nothing that could be put into words in the first place.

Silence does not necessarily mean they could not think of something clever. Once the grip is laid out and looked at, there was very little to say. In that moment nobody could assemble a sentence.

* * *

What Zhaozhou does when he comes back is worth more time.

He does not comment on the day's business. He takes off his shoe, puts it on his head, and leaves. A shoe belongs on a foot. Put on the head, it is upside down. The gesture seems to say: it is not right to call the killing completely senseless, and yet adding another cut in order to cut off the quarrel is one more layer of the same struggle. Even “stop arguing” can be clenched into a position to defend.

Nanquan says that if Zhaozhou had been there, the cat would have been saved. That is probably the point. The cat would not have needed to be killed. A person usually gets the thought *it has to be mine* first, and only then quarrels that hard over a cat. If the thought loosens, who it belongs to stops mattering so much.

* * *

The things we quarrel about day to day are often like this: who got the last word, whose name goes on the credit. Looking back afterward, it is usually hard to say what the fight was really about, yet the force of it was very real, and even “I am right” would not be let go.

What needs to be untied is probably not out there in the stroke itself.

---

**Last time you argued until your face was hot, were you arguing about the thing, or about the sentence in your head that said “I have to win”?**
