> Xiangyan Zhixian came to study with Guishan. Guishan said, “I hear that under our late teacher Baizhang you answered ten for every one question, a hundred for every ten. That is your cleverness, your understanding and thinking — the root of birth and death. Try to say one sentence from before your parents were born.”
>
> Xiangyan had no idea what to say. He begged Guishan again and again to explain it. Guishan said, “What I said would be mine. It would never have anything to do with you.”
>
> Xiangyan left. Years later, clearing weeds, he threw a piece of broken tile. It struck a bamboo and made a sound, and he woke up.

* * *

Xiangyan had been sharp under Baizhang. One question, ten answers. Everyone around him said he was intelligent.

Then Guishan asks one question: say a sentence from before your parents gave birth to you.

Xiangyan cannot. He goes back and searches everything he has written down over the years and finds nothing. Several times he asks Guishan to explain. Guishan refuses. What I said would be mine, he tells him, and would never be yours.

So Xiangyan leaves. Later he is weeding in the hills, tosses a piece of tile without thinking, and it hits bamboo. One sound, and suddenly he understands.

* * *

Guishan's question does not look like an examination. “Before your parents were born” cannot be pointed at. There is nothing to see or hear that it could rest on. The skill of answering ten for one, which worked everywhere else, does not come up here at all.

A person who is good with answers usually freezes at this question. The more smoothly he has answered all his life, the less he can find to say now. The sentences he has memorized seem to step back out of the way.

Xiangyan keeps asking Guishan to “explain it.” He probably wants one more sentence that he can remember and use. Guishan will not give it, not even a sentence to point the way. It looks unkind. But if he had said it then, the sound of the bamboo years later would probably not have been Xiangyan's to hear.

* * *

A tile hitting bamboo is not a mysterious event. You are working in the field, your hand throws something without thinking, there is a sound, and the sound is gone. You were not prepared to listen. You did not intend to hear anything in it.

And it happens in exactly that gap. At that moment there is no question waiting to be answered, no examination paper to hand in. You have not even had time to think, *this must be the moment I understand*. You are simply doing the work in front of you.

A person whose mind is always full seems to need an unexpected gap. There may not be an answer hidden in the gap. It is more that the mind which has been busy looking for answers did not catch up in time, and for once you had the space to let the sound in.

**Is there something that became clear only after you stopped trying to understand it?**
