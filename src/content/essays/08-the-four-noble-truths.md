The day my colleague got his physical exam results, he stared at the sheet for a long time. He pointed at a line of small print and asked whether it was a problem. The doctor said it was within the normal range. He was relieved, and then gloomy all afternoon.

I thought of the Buddha's opening line, "there is suffering in a human life." I had never quite understood it. That day I understood a little: suffering does not wait until the pain is unbearable.

The **Four Noble Truths** are the first stone in the foundation. They are not dogma for you to accept. They are observations you can check, one by one, against your own experience.

![Suffering is not for understanding](/illustrations/ill-009.jpg)

## The list

There is suffering. Said plainly, that is not pessimism. It is a description. The Buddha noticed that anything put together by conditions carries certain marks:

- **Birth, aging, death**
- **Sorrow, lamentation, pain**
- **Being joined to what you dislike**
- **Being parted from what you love**
- **Not getting what you want**

Anyone who reads the list can match a few items to their own life. It is not an instruction to give up. It is an instruction to stop looking away. **See it clearly first; only then is there anything to do.**

## Craving

Suffering arises from **craving** (tanha): craving for pleasure, craving for existence, craving for nonexistence. The root of that craving is not seeing things as they are. Everyone knows the afternoon when nothing is missing and something is still off. That is the feeling. Those are the hands reaching with nowhere to grab.

If suffering comes from craving, then when craving settles, suffering settles. That ease is called **nirvana**. You do not have to go anywhere. The hand opens.

The way it settles is the **Eightfold Path**, a set of steps that fit into an ordinary day. The eight are not eight separate roads. They are eight faces of one thing: how to think, how to speak, how to act, how to settle the mind.

## Not a theory

Chan masters keep repeating one point: the Four Noble Truths are not ideas suspended in the air. You have to walk through them yourself. Put more directly: **the truth of suffering is not for understanding. It is for seeing.**

My colleague's report is still folded in his notebook. The report is fine. What the report cannot see is the unease it left behind.
