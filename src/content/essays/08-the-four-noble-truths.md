The Four Noble Truths form the cornerstone of Buddhist philosophy. They are not doctrines to be believed, but observations to be verified through one's own experience.

## The First Noble Truth: Dukkha (Suffering)

Life involves suffering. This is not pessimism — it is realism. The Buddha observed that all conditioned existence is marked by:

- **Birth, aging, and death**
- **Sorrow, lamentation, and despair**
- **Union with what is displeasing**
- **Separation from what is pleasing**
- **Not getting what one wants**

## The Second Noble Truth: The Origin of Suffering

Suffering arises from craving (tanha) — the thirst for pleasure, existence, and non-existence. This craving is rooted in ignorance of the true nature of reality.

## The Third Noble Truth: The Cessation of Suffering

If suffering arises from craving, then by eliminating craving, we can eliminate suffering. This state of freedom is called Nirvana.

## The Fourth Noble Truth: The Path to the Cessation of Suffering

The way to end suffering is the Noble Eightfold Path — a practical framework for living that leads to liberation.

## In the Chan Tradition

Chan masters often emphasize that the Four Noble Truths are not abstract philosophy but direct experience. As one master put it: "The truth of suffering is not something to be understood — it is something to be seen."
