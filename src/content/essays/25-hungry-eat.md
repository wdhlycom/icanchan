A Vinaya master once asked Dazhu Huihai: do you still have to make an effort, practicing the Way?

Huihai said he did.

How?

When hungry, eat. When tired, sleep.

The master was puzzled. How is that any different from what everyone else does?

Huihai said: it isn't the same. They won't eat when they eat — a hundred demands. They won't sleep when they sleep — a thousand calculations.

![When Hungry, Eat](/illustrations/ill-111.jpg)
The hinge of that exchange isn't in eating and sleeping. That part is too ordinary to argue about; anyone manages it. The hinge is the last line.

You eat with the phone face up beside the plate, your head running the two o'clock meeting, your mouth chewing something it never names. You lie down and the body is down, but the mind is still in an argument with someone from the afternoon. That is probably what *won't* means. We won't stay where we actually are.

* * *

So the sentence doesn't land as a comfort. It sets a hard standard: body and mind in the same place.

Hunger is the body raising a hand. Eating is the body answering. But people are often hungry and not willing to eat, and eating and not willing to be full — chewing while budgeting, worrying, replaying a remark from Tuesday. The body sits at the table, the mind has gone somewhere else, and the two have come apart.

What practice asks for sounds unremarkable: go and fetch the mind, and stand it next to the body.

* * *

It is harder now than it was in Huihai's day. The food arrives and someone photographs it first. A few bites, then a reply to a message. Halfway through, two short videos. By the end, most people could not say what they ate.

Part of the trouble is that a meal has become one more item to get through between two other items. The plate is there, the food is there, but the attention is booked solid somewhere else, and the eating runs underneath it like a background process.

That isn't a small thing. A person who can't be present at a plate of food is probably not present on the cushion either. The posture is right, the legs are folded, and the mind is out in the hallway.

* * *

Some people get there by the least dramatic route available. Phone in another room, out of reach. Just eating. One bite is one bite. The tongue meets the salt, the teeth test how soft or hard the thing is. The mind wanders off toward a deadline, you see it go, and you walk it back. It wanders again. You walk it back again.

Nothing mystical in that. It is taking four words and doing them once, properly.

**A question to sit with:** When did you last taste what was actually in your mouth?
