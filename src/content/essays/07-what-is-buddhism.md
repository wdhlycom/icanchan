Buddhism is one of the world's major spiritual traditions, founded by Siddhartha Gautama — the Buddha — in ancient India around 2,500 years ago. At its core, Buddhism is not a religion in the conventional sense, but a practical philosophy and way of life aimed at understanding the nature of suffering and finding liberation from it.

## The Buddha's Discovery

The Buddha's teaching began with a simple but profound observation: life involves suffering. But he didn't stop there. Through deep meditation and insight, he discovered that suffering has causes, and that by addressing those causes, one can find freedom.

## Core Teachings

The Buddha's teachings can be summarized in several key frameworks:

- **The Four Noble Truths**: The diagnosis and cure for the human condition
- **The Eightfold Path**: The practical way to liberation
- **Dependent Origination**: Understanding how everything arises in connection
- **The Three Marks of Existence**: Impermanence, unsatisfactoriness, and non-self

## Why It Matters Today

In an age of anxiety and distraction, the Buddha's teachings offer a timeless framework for understanding our minds and finding peace. The Chan tradition, in particular, emphasizes direct experience over doctrine — making it especially relevant for modern seekers.
