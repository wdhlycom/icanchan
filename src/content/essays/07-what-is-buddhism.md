Buddhism often begins with four terms: the **Four Noble Truths**, the **Eightfold Path**, **dependent origination**, and the **three marks**. Put them side by side and they look like four prescriptions taped to the same wall. What illness were they written for?

## Diagnose before prescribing

Buddhism is one of the major spiritual traditions, and it starts about twenty-six hundred years ago with Siddhartha Gautama, later called the Buddha. But it is not a religion in the usual sense. It is closer to a set of instructions for living: see how **suffering** accumulates, and how a person gets out of it becomes visible.

Find the illness first. Then talk about medicine. The Buddha's opening line is as plain as it gets: there is suffering in a human life.

He did not stop there. He sat and watched for a long time and saw that suffering has causes. Once the causes are seen and treated, a person can breathe again. **The step from "there is suffering" to "suffering has a cause" turns resignation into something you can work with.**

## The four lists

What he taught can roughly be sorted into the four terms:

- **Four Noble Truths**: diagnose the human situation, then prescribe.
- **Eightfold Path**: the specific steps toward release.
- **Dependent origination**: how things arise by leaning on one another.
- **Three marks**: impermanence, suffering, no-self.

The names were organized later. The Buddha did not teach from a chart. He took one case at a time: what is this, where does it come from, how does it stop. The lists look like four walls. They are gestures pointing in the same direction: **go and look for yourself**.

## Why it still works

People are anxious and distracted now. Messages arrive faster than they can be read, and the unease underneath keeps turning. The teaching still works, not because it asks for belief, but because it asks you to see the mind clearly and settle it.

Chan is especially insistent on verification. Open the koan collections and the masters answer sideways, refusing to hand over something memorizable. That is oddly suited to the present: **it does not live your life for you. It makes you look.**

Do not memorize the four words yet. Notice where your body is tight right now. You already know.
