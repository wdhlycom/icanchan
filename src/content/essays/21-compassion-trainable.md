The word gets used as though it names a temperament. Some people come into the world soft, others hard, and there is not much to be done about which one you were issued. I held that view for years without examining it, partly because it was convenient. If compassion is a temperament, the people who lack it are simply unlucky, and nobody has to try.

The research does not divide people that way. In trials where people are taught to practice compassion meditation, activity in the brain regions connected with empathy and with care increases little by little over the training. The usual comparison is a muscle. The part you use grows; the part you leave alone recedes. Use it or lose it, applied to something we normally file under character.

## The older definition

What I had not expected is that the older definition was never about temperament either. In Chan, ci originally means giving happiness, and bei means pulling suffering out. Neither word names a mood. The starting point sounds less like a feeling than a motion. First you are willing for your own suffering to end. Then that willingness gets extended, a little at a time, to people close to you, to people you have never met, and finally to the ones you find difficult — the neighbor, the colleague, the relative whose calls you let ring.

That is not the same as being soft-hearted, which is what I had been confusing it with. Soft-heartedness is the inability to watch someone suffer. The chest tightens, and there the matter ends. Compassion watches the same scene and then reaches out a hand. The difference is not in the volume of feeling. It is in whether anything happens next.

---

Here is the part that sounded wrong to me at first. The practice almost always begins with yourself. A person who spends the day prosecuting himself, who will not let himself off a single charge, is unlikely to have much gentleness left for anyone else. The willingness has to exist somewhere before it can be passed on. You have to be willing for your own suffering to stop, and only then can that willingness be handed to another person unchanged. This is not selfishness with better manners. It is closer to a spring: water needs a source before it can run anywhere.

The order matters more than it sounds. Most of us try the reverse. We are patient with strangers — we hold the door, cover a shift, absorb a colleague's bad afternoon — and then come home with nothing left for the people who will still be there tomorrow. The account has been drawn down all day and nobody thought to put anything into it. Beginning with yourself is not a detour around other people. It is the only way the giving has something behind it.

I notice the failure most in the negative. On the days when I have been harsh with myself over a missed deadline, a sentence that came out wrong, a thing I should have finished last week, the people around me get the remainder, and the remainder is thin. The trouble is not that I do not care about them. It is that there was nothing in the account to draw on.

So the order is not a piece of self-help advice bolted onto an old teaching. It is the shape of the teaching. The willingness has to be real somewhere before it can travel, and the only place it can start being real is close to home, where you can see the whole mess and still decide not to add to it.

## A road, not a gift

None of this makes the practice easy, and the trials do not promise that it will be. What they describe is a direction, not a speed: the capacity responds to use, on the same ordinary terms as a muscle responds to use. It does not promise that the response arrives fast, or that it feels like anything while it is happening. Most of it probably does not.

So the claim I end up with is smaller than the one I started with, and more useful. Compassion is not entirely a gift handed out at birth. It is closer to a road that can be walked step by step, and the pace is not the point. The road was never fast. What matters is that the direction exists, and that the first step is available even on a bad day — the small, unglamorous willingness to let your own afternoon be a little less of a punishment. From there it can go outward. In my experience it generally does, and not on any schedule I chose.
