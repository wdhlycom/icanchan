> Mazu Daoyi was living at Chuanfa Monastery and sat in meditation every day. Huairang recognized that he was a vessel for the Dharma and went to ask him, “What are you aiming at, sitting there?”
>
> Mazu said, “I am aiming to become a Buddha.”
>
> Huairang picked up a tile and began grinding it on a stone in front of the hut.
>
> Mazu asked, “What are you doing?”
>
> “Polishing it into a mirror.”
>
> “How can grinding a tile make a mirror?”
>
> Huairang said, “If grinding a tile cannot make a mirror, can sitting in meditation make a Buddha?”

* * *

Mazu sat every day, and no one could talk him out of it.

Huairang arrives and asks what the sitting is for. *To become a Buddha.* Huairang says nothing more. He picks up a tile and squats in front of the hut, grinding it on a stone. Mazu watches for a while and asks what he is doing. *Making a mirror.*

*How can a tile become a mirror?*

Huairang answers: if a tile cannot become a mirror, can sitting become a Buddha?

* * *

The power of the question is not that it says sitting is wrong.

Huairang does not say meditation is bad. He does not say Mazu is applying himself incorrectly. He takes the three words “aiming at” out of Mazu's own mouth and sets a tile down beside him.

A tile and a mirror are not the same kind of thing. Grind as long as you like and it will not become one. Once you treat the thing in your hands as a token to be exchanged for some other result, you gradually stop looking at what the thing itself is like. Sitting is sitting. A Buddha is probably not a finished product ground out that way.

* * *

There are a lot of tiles like this in ordinary life.

You read in order to get a score, and somewhere along the way the book stops having any taste. You exercise with your eyes on the number on the scale, and by the end you are not paying attention to whether your body feels well. You are kind to someone hoping for something in return, and when the return does not come, the kindness changes flavor.

None of these activities is wrong. What gets added is one word: *for*. With that one word added, only half of what you are doing is actually happening. The other half is hanging in the air, waiting to be cashed in.

Huairang grinds the tile for Mazu to watch. The tile stays a tile. After all that grinding, nothing has been produced. And the point seems to be precisely in that nothing. No further explanation is needed. Mazu sees it himself.

---

**If the thing you are doing right now were not for exchanging into something else, what would be left of it?**
