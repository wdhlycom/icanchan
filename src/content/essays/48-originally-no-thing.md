Bodhi has no tree. The bright mirror has no stand. Originally there is not one thing — so where could dust collect?

Plenty of people can say Hui Neng's verse. Reciting it doesn't disperse much. Which raises the obvious question: if originally there is not one thing, where did this pile in the chest come from?

![Originally There Is Nothing](/illustrations/ill-097.jpg)
The line probably isn't a claim that people don't suffer. It is closer to an account of how suffering gets made, and what it is made of.

Trouble doesn't sit in you like a stone you can't shift. It gets stacked. Something happens; at first that is all it is, an event. Then a thought arrives and labels it — *this is a disaster, I am finished* — and only after the label does it become the knot.

* * *

The same sentence from someone who doesn't matter is heard and set down. From someone you love, it gets turned over all night. The words didn't change. The listener added weight.

It works the same way with events. A meeting goes badly, a message goes unanswered, a plan slips by a week. In themselves these are small pieces of information. A tone of voice, a door closed a little harder than usual, a reply that arrives an hour late — none of it carries a verdict until someone hands it one. It is the commentary running on top of them, hour after hour, that turns them into something you carry around in the body.

That's the shape of it. There is no solid object called the problem that was sitting there all along. It was assembled, layer on layer, and the verse is pointing at the bottom of the stack: it looks heavy, and underneath there is nothing being gripped.

* * *

Which can sound like a dismissal of pain. Hui Neng didn't say suffering was fake. What he seems to have refused is the idea that suffering is one solid block with weight and edges that cannot be moved.

Because if it is empty, assembled, then what can be assembled can also come apart. That is the practical half of the verse.

* * *

Trouble arrives, and most of us ask why this keeps happening to me, or else recite the line a few times and try to press the feeling flat. Neither does much. Another way is slower. Don't press. Look.

Is this a fact, or did I supply it?

This *I am finished* — is that going to happen, or am I frightening myself?

One layer seen clearly, and the chest gives a little. The verse is something you see your way down, layer by layer. Said out loud with nothing behind it, it is only syllables.

**A question to sit with:** The thing sitting heaviest in you right now — is it what happened, or the story your thoughts have been adding to all week?
