> A monk asked Zhaozhou, “What was the meaning of Bodhidharma coming from the West?”
>
> Zhaozhou said, “The cypress tree in the courtyard.”
>
> “Do not use scenery to answer me, teacher.”
>
> “I am not using scenery to answer you.”
>
> “What was the meaning of Bodhidharma coming from the West?”
>
> “The cypress tree in the courtyard.”

* * *

The monk wants a *meaning*: the sense behind the words, the origin, the point of the whole thing. In his mind, that has to be chased upward and outward. The more remote and mysterious it sounds, the more it resembles an answer.

Zhaozhou gives him a tree. It does not carry a doctrine, it does not bring a history. It is simply standing in the yard.

The line “do not use scenery” gives the monk away. What is visible right here apparently does not count. It has to be somewhere else. We do the same thing when we read a passage or meet a situation: we assume a deeper meaning is hidden behind it, and we let the layer in front of us pass by. To this monk, the tree in the yard has probably become the least worthy thing in the conversation.

Zhaozhou does not argue and does not revise. The second time, he says exactly the same. That closes off the road of *ask again and maybe he will give me something deep*. The monk has nowhere left to go.

* * *

One thing is easy to miss: Zhaozhou does not say the tree *is* the answer. He says the tree, and adds nothing to it. He does not make it mean anything.

A tree is a tree. It only becomes an evasion because the monk insists it must stand for something. Koans are hard, and it is not always because the words are too deep. Often the listener arrives with a sieve. What is concrete, present, and needs no explanation gets filtered out before it is even looked at.

A lot of everyday dissatisfaction comes from the same place. The food is on the table, the person is across from you, the work in your hands is underway, and some part of you keeps feeling that the thing that really deserves attention is somewhere else.

---

**If the thing in front of you did not have to stand for anything else, what would it be like on its own?**
