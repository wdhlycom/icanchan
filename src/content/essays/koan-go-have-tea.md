> The master asked a newly arrived monk, “Have you been here before?”
>
> “I have.”
>
> “Go have some tea.”
>
> He asked another monk, “Have you been here before?”
>
> “I have not.”
>
> “Go have some tea.”
>
> The head of the monastery asked, “Why tell the one who has been here to have tea, and the one who has not been here to have tea?”
>
> The master called, “Head of the monastery.”
>
> “Yes?”
>
> “Go have some tea.”

Three people, three different situations, and Zhaozhou answers all of them with the same three words.

* * *

It does not sound like a clever exchange, and it does not sound like he is brushing them off. He does not seem to be holding a set of answers to hand out.

The monk who had been there before was probably carrying a little pride: *I am an old hand.* The one who had not was probably carrying a little nervousness: *I have only just arrived.* And the abbot, watching, was in a hurry for a proper explanation. Each of the three had something set up in front of him, and each was waiting for Zhaozhou to give an account.

Zhaozhou's line does not sort who came early and who came late. It does not rule on whether the question should have been asked. It moves all three of them onto the same thing: drinking tea.

* * *

“Go have some tea” is hard exactly because it is so ordinary. People come to a teacher hoping to hear something deep that they can copy into a notebook. What Zhaozhou gives is something anybody can do: tea.

And because it is ordinary, it stings a little. It offers no doctrine. It pulls a person out of the need to be given an account and back to the cup in his hand. The cup is warm. The person is sitting down. One swallow, and whether he had been there before, whether he understood, matters less than it did a moment ago.

Seen this way, practice is not a matter of how much you know. It is mostly a willingness to take the thing in front of you and carry it through, steadily.

---

**When your head is full of “what should I be doing,” can you just finish the one thing in front of you, quietly?**
