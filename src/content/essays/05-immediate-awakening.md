I saw a face in a crowd that I had not seen in years. My mouth opened, and the name arrived two or three seconds later. The face I recognized at once. Later I remembered the Chan line "nothing but water, nowhere else ice," and the moment started to look interesting: recognizing and remembering are not the same act.

Chan splits into **gradual** and **sudden**. The gradual school says awakening is built slowly, step by step, year by year, until the mind is cleaned out. The sudden school, associated with Huineng, says something different. Awakening is not a place to arrive at. It is more like recognizing something you have already seen, and it can happen now.

![Nothing but water, nowhere else ice](/illustrations/Zen36.jpg)

Put in modern cognitive terms, the gradual school describes **training**: patience, repetition, the slow cultivation of attention. The sudden school describes an **event**: the moment that training meets the right condition and clicks. Those are not the same claim, so they do not contradict each other.

## What sudden really says

The important part of the sudden teaching is this: awakening is not an achievement you manufacture. It is not accumulated. It is not built toward. You **recognize** it, the way you recognize a familiar face, or the way you suddenly see the answer to a problem you have been circling. The answer was there. The recognition takes a second. The conditions that make that second possible take years.

> Awakening is like water and ice. Apart from water there is no ice. (Hakuin Ekaku)

The image is precise. Ice is not a second substance produced by water. Ice **is** water, colder and frozen. Awakening is not a new thing practice manufactures. It **is** this mind, in another form. Practice cannot produce awakening. What practice produces is the conditions in which awakening can be recognized.

## No need to become anything

You do not have to become something first. You do not have to fix yourself, improve, or climb. What is asked is only to **see**. This seeing is available now, in this sentence, in this breath, in the ordinary fact of being awake and not noticing it.

The sudden school puts it strongly: what stands between you and clarity is the thought that something stands between you and clarity.

The face passed me and went on. I did not go over and say hello. Recognition does not require saying anything.
