When Nanquan's *ordinary mind is the Way* got around, plenty of people were confused by it. If the point is ordinary mind, do we still need diligence? Do we still get up and practice? Can we simply do as we please from here on?

![Ordinary Mind, Steady Effort](/illustrations/ill-124.jpg)
That is hearing ordinary mind as *whatever mind*.

Ordinary mind doesn't look like slouching or drifting. It looks more like the mind after the effort has been spent, no longer gnawing on what is finished. Diligence covers the first half: you are willing to put the work in. Ordinary mind covers the second: when it is done, you can set it down. They sound like two separate things. In one person they usually arrive together.

* * *

The old teachers make this plain enough. Plenty of them put in brutal effort. Some wore out their cushions. Some carried a single phrase for years without cracking it and kept going. And none of them seem to have walked around with *I want to become something* held in the chest.

Diligence is the feet not stopping. Ordinary mind is the string not being pulled so hard. Someone who only pushes, with the string taut for years, tends to end up practicing bitterly and tightly. Someone who only talks about being ordinary while the hands stay still has usually found a respectable word for resting. Both failures are plain from the outside, and nearly invisible from where the person is sitting.

* * *

What Chan describes is doing the work with full effort and not wrestling with a result that hasn't arrived.

Sitting. Koan work. Whatever is on the desk. Take your turn at each and skip none of it. Only while doing it, don't hang the hook that says *this has to succeed, this has to win*. And when it is finished, put it down without going back to check it ten times.

The checking is where most of the exhaustion lives. The task itself takes an hour. Going back over it afterward, in the shower and on the walk home and at two in the morning, can take the rest of the day.

* * *

Which is why ordinary mind and diligence don't actually fight.

Ordinary mind is the unhurried look that shows up after the effort has mostly been spent. Where effort was needed, it was given, so the mind quiets and stops feeling it owes something to somebody. Nothing is left over to defend, and nothing is being saved up for a verdict later.

* * *

So when the question comes around again — should I be pushing harder — some people stop choosing between the two ends. The feet keep working. The mind keeps learning to sit loose. Neither half gets dropped, and neither half gets promoted into a philosophy.

---

**A question to sit with:** In your diligence, how much is really wanting the work done well, and how much is the panic that arrives the moment you stop?
