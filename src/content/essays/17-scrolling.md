People who cannot stop scrolling are not short on willpower. The trouble is that the gesture happens a hundred times a day: you pick up the phone to check the time, look up, and twenty minutes are gone. You put it down feeling emptier than when you picked it up. It is not a moral failing. It is a habit with a very short loop, and short loops are hard to see.

The problem is not entirely the phone. It is the small gap before each reach.

![Why You Can't Stop Scrolling](/illustrations/ill-064.jpg)
Chan has a phrase for the mind: the monkey. It swings from one branch to the next, one video to the next. You look like you are watching. Look again and you are hiding, hiding from the silence where there is nothing to hold and nothing to disturb.

The three minutes while the food comes. The five after the lights go out. The short wait in line. In those gaps nothing arrives, and staying there is uncomfortable. So the phone comes out, and the next bite fills the space.

The space never fills. The more you scroll the emptier it gets, and the emptier it gets the more you scroll. It is the reverse of *when hungry, eat*. Hunger is natural; one bowl and you are done. The thirst fed bite by bite only grows.

* * *

So the work is not necessarily quitting the phone. It is seeing the thought *one more scroll* rise.

When it rises, do not rush to follow it. You will notice the urge behaves like a wave. It swells, it recedes. You do not have to fight it. You just do not add the next bite. Sometimes it passes in ten seconds and you are still standing in the kitchen, phone face down, slightly surprised.

That pause is the same gap as the one in anger. Anger has a gap that flares loudly and takes the room with it. Scrolling has one that takes the time away quietly, so quietly that you only notice afterward, when the evening is gone.

* * *

Before reaching for the phone, someone runs one line through the mind: am I wanting to look at something, or to hide from something?

Ask it and let it go. No verdict. If you still want to scroll, scroll. But for that moment you are the one who sees the scrolling, not the one being scrolled away.

That is the whole training. Each time, let the line finish before the hand moves. It will not work every time. Each time, though, the gap moves a little from *must escape* to *can stay*.

---

**A question to sit with:** At the moment you most want to scroll today, a few seconds earlier, what were you doing, or was that gap never empty at all?
